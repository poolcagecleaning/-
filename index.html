Our Pool Cage Cleaning Process — Step by Step

Consistency is what separates a great cleaning from a mediocre one. Our service follows a structured, repeatable process on every job so nothing is missed and the results hold up over time.

  1. 1
    Pre-Inspection & Site Prep

    We walk the full perimeter of the enclosure to assess contamination levels, identify any pre-existing screen damage, and note proximity to pool water, plants, or outdoor furniture that needs protection during the clean.

  2. 2
    Dry Debris Removal

    Leaves, spider webs, insect nests, and loose organic matter are removed by hand and with soft brushes before any liquid is applied. This prevents wet debris from smearing across screens during washing.

  3. 3
    Soft-Wash Solution Application

    Our biodegradable cleaning solution is applied to all screen panels and aluminum framing at low pressure. The formula is left to dwell for several minutes, breaking down mold, mildew, algae, and oxidation chemically rather than mechanically.

  4. 4
    Precision Rinse

    Panels are rinsed top-to-bottom with controlled low-pressure water, flushing all dissolved contamination away from screens, framing, and the pool deck below.

  5. 5
    Frame & Track Detail

    Aluminum extrusions, corner joints, door frames, and spline tracks are wiped and spot-cleaned by hand to remove residue that rinse water alone does not fully clear.

  6. 6
    Final Walk-Through

    We inspect the completed work panel by panel before we leave, confirming every section is clean, streak-free, and structurally undisturbed.

What We Clean — Full Pool Cage & Lanai Scope

Pool cage cleaning is more involved than it may appear from the outside. A complete enclosure includes dozens of individual components, each of which accumulates contaminants differently and requires attention:

Screen Panels

The mesh panels are the primary focus of any lanai cleaning job. Over time, they collect algae (which appears as a greenish-grey film), mold and mildew (appearing as black or dark brown spotting), salt deposits, calcium residue from pool splash, pollen, and fine dust. All of these reduce light transmission and make the enclosure feel dark and uninviting. Our soft-wash process removes the full organic layer and restores screen clarity without stretching or tearing the mesh.

Aluminum Framing & Extrusions

The structural aluminum members — top plates, horizontal rails, vertical posts, and corner connectors — oxidize in Naples' salt-air environment, developing a white chalky film called aluminum oxide. Left untreated, this accelerates corrosion and discolors the surrounding screens. We treat oxidized framing with targeted cleaners that break down the chalky layer and restore the profile's original finish.

Doors, Hinges & Hardware

Screen doors and their hardware are high-traffic areas that attract dirt, insect debris, and rust from the hinge screws. We clean door panels individually and address the frame, sweeps, and hinge areas to ensure the full entry point is as clean as the surrounding enclosure.

Interior Soffit & Roof Panels

The overhead screen panels of a pool cage are often the most neglected — and the most visually impactful when dirty. Interior soffits collect mold, pollen, and spider webs that rain down into the pool and onto outdoor furniture. We clean overhead panels with care for the water and surfaces below.

How Often Should You Clean Your Pool Cage in Naples?

The frequency of professional pool cage cleaning depends on your property's microenvironment. Homes near tidal water, backing up to preserve areas, or located under large trees will accumulate biological debris faster than properties in more open settings. As a general guideline for the Naples area:

  • Every 6 months — Properties near water, heavy tree canopy, or with known mold issues.
  • Annually — Typical Naples residential properties in open or semi-open settings.
  • After major storms — Post-hurricane or heavy storm cleaning to remove debris, organic matter, and salt spray deposited during the event.

Regular professional cleaning does more than maintain appearance. It is preventive maintenance. Screen mesh that stays clean lasts significantly longer than mesh that sits saturated with organic acids from algae and mold — meaning routine cleaning extends the life of your screens and delays costly re-screening projects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will soft washing damage my pool water or surrounding plants?

No. Our cleaning solutions are biodegradable and pH-balanced. We cover pool returns and sensitive plants before application as a precaution, and the dilution achieved during rinsing presents no risk to pool chemistry or landscape vegetation.

How long does a typical pool cage cleaning take?

Most residential pool cages in Naples take between 1.5 and 3 hours, depending on size, contamination level, and the number of panels. Larger estate cages or enclosures with heavy mold or oxidation may take longer.

Do I need to be home during the cleaning?

Not necessarily. As long as we have access to an outdoor water source and the gate to the pool area is unlocked or you can arrange access, the job can be completed without you on-site. We will contact you before and after for peace of mind.

Can you clean my lanai screens without cleaning the whole pool cage?

Yes. If your primary concern is the lanai panel area rather than the full cage, we can focus our service there. We will assess whether isolated cleaning makes sense given the overall state of the enclosure and advise accordingly.

What if some of my screens are already torn or damaged before you arrive?

We document any pre-existing damage during our initial walk-through and flag it for you. We can clean around damaged sections and, depending on the extent, recommend whether repair or re-screening should happen before or after cleaning.

Is this service available year-round in Naples?

Yes. We operate throughout the year. Many customers schedule a clean before season (October–November) to arrive at a fresh enclosure, and another after season or following summer storm activity.
